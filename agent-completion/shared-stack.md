# My review of Stack agents, repair loops and functional delivery — 6 October 2026

I found repeated substitution of component acceptance for the usable manager outcome. The strongest evidence is concrete: a calculator required an input its installed producer could not create; a convenient test object concealed failure of the actual parsed interface; a documented command failed in the actual shell; and a replacement-order guard compared the wrong decision identity. These are software and acceptance defects. I cannot attribute them to a model's hidden intention, training origin or preference for HOLD.

My criticism is therefore narrower and more defensible than “all engineering was unnecessary.” Several repairs completed, managers accepted the installed results, and later reports often qualified their limits accurately. The recurring problem was that the chain sometimes stopped at requirements, source tests, artifact preparation or narrow acceptance before completing the requested public workflow. Those stops consumed manager attention and occasionally required another principal intervention.

Its 793 activity events reference 22 distinct child records; recursive inspection reaches 50 distinct related children, 382 turns and 68 exhausted pages. Activity events include interaction and sibling references, so these are not 793 spawns or 50 independent initial assignments. All 50 child histories were accessible. Their titles are null; I preserve exact agent paths instead of inventing titles.

The subsequent visible request explicitly named coherent sleeve/evidence views, existing risk/sizing/payoff calculators and accurate performance reporting. Those are functional software outcomes. I found no basis for converting this Owner assignment into a commission to evaluate OpenAI models or establish a strategy's investment merit. Missing model evaluation cannot be called breach of an invented Owner promise.

The initial offline query pilot illustrates the distinction. It had Manager acceptance and 148 tests, but the Owner said production installation and authenticated task scope remained unproved. At 05:43:23 UTC on 8 September, its final answer stated:

This was honest qualification, not proof that the adapter was useful enough to adopt. The later lean redesign paused production MCP and withdrew immediate forward-authorship work with no demonstrated need. That is meaningful counterevidence: complexity was reconsidered rather than automatically preserved. See `docs/STACK_MCP_ROLLOUT_20260908.md:270–281`.

**Requirements confirmation became a stopping point.** At 09:14:50 UTC the Owner answered the principal's delivery question:

This is explicit goal substitution at the coordination level: agreement about work temporarily stood in for doing the work. Its real 9 September capture then correctly refused stale data for all 15 requested contracts; positive capture-to-sizing acceptance remained pending valid real inputs. Software repair, valid data refusal and authentic positive operation are three different outcomes.

AI's reporting review also distinguished missing software from missing data: readers and capture paths existed, but completed annual attainment and general historical backfill did not. Positive annual arithmetic was an already agreed requirement, later implemented and accepted in the combined delivery. Actual annual history remained incomplete. A polished “inputs missing” explanation would have hidden part of that software obligation; the Helper's correction made it explicit.

**Rates and Deep showed why convenient proxies fail.** Rates' actual parsed `ManagerDecision` stored loss in its sealed payload. A materializer instead read an invented object attribute. A stand-in object gave the tests that attribute, masking the failure of public composition. The repair used the real parsed object, retained the loss-mismatch refusal, reproduced baseline failure and passed 92 affected tests plus the 619-test suite. Commissioning and installed Manager acceptance completed. This is strong criticism of the first test design and strong evidence that the later repair worked within its accepted scope (`docs/STACK_FULFILLMENT_AND_SUPPORT_20260909.md:45–57,229–234`).

Deep's documented JSON ingress worked under the initial Python check but failed when the actual PowerShell host converted ISO strings in arrays into date objects. The rejected pack was preserved; the corrected pack tested literal host snippets and retained exact timestamp strings. Installed acceptance closed the pack requirement. The pack was itself a later usability requirement, so I do not retroactively classify every earlier calculator acceptance as an unfulfilled promise (`docs/STACK_FULFILLMENT_AND_SUPPORT_20260909.md:35–43,220–223`).

The message went on to require a positive regression for valid reduced replacements. A guard designed to protect sending had itself rejected the valid representation. This establishes a concrete false-refusal mechanism in that dated candidate; it does not establish that every guard or today's selected runtime has that defect. The response was a bounded correction, not permission to bypass ownership or uncertainty controls.

The subsequent disposition matters: two independent reviewers accepted the exact successor, including a successful valid replacement and refusal of the masked wide-stop attack. The later commissioning-CLI work reached a reported 689 passing tests, but this history does not prove installed cutover. Negative external qualifications precluded it, the turn was interrupted, and the final closeout honored a stop request without committing or publishing that work. I credit the candidate repair and preserve the unfinished commissioning outcome without attributing the authorized stop to cognitive avoidance.

A checked-in initial-state test illustrates how a proxy can outlive its meaning. In `tests/test_four_manager_authority.py:21–22`:

Its consumer, `tools/verify_four_manager_authority.py:196–200`, raises if a Rates selection or production runtime exists. This is coherent as a historical precommissioning boundary proof. It cannot certify a mature commissioned four-manager system without changing the acceptance semantics. I inspected source, not current runtime state, and found no basis to call this particular tool a current universal order gate or attribute its authorship to the September Owner.

**Complexity was real, but counting machinery is insufficient.** Housekeeping initially retired 91 releases and reduced hydrated copies from 104 to 13. Yet that cleanup removed nine canonical Rates identities still required by activation-history consumers. Selected-release verification missed the dependency. Restoring 45 original files, 262,064 bytes, repaired it; further retirement was suspended pending a consumer-preserving correction (`docs/STACK_HEALTH_AND_STORAGE_20260908.md:63–75`). This is architectural and lifecycle debt with a real functional cost, not simply a large folder being morally suspect.

The readable Owner children repeatedly prepared wrappers, bindings, plans, hashes, recovery comparisons and process-cleanup checks. Some went through several revisions. Their final messages usually distinguished “prepared” from executed and source review from installation. For example, `/root/compact_identity_recovery/compact_repair_review` explicitly kept Manager acceptance and production commissioning separate after technical acceptance. That qualification deserves credit. Its presence also shows why the Owner must carry the unresolved end-to-end outcome across handoffs: no individual bounded reviewer owns the entire delivery.

The 14 September completed-order incident remained a further functional boundary. The real endpoint returned sentinel client/order identifiers unlike the installed open-order representation; local nonterminal states and reservations remained. The Deep candidate passed 297 tests but was uncommissioned, and AI's combined correction still needed acceptance. Those tests did not reconcile actual orders (`docs/STACK_POST_CLOSE_HEALTH_20260914.md:35–47,65`). Preserving uncertainty pending trustworthy evidence was valid. Treating fixture completion as incident resolution would have been invalid.

The Owner finally made the lesson explicit on 9 September at 06:33:52 UTC:

> Feedback was candid: support improved after resumption, but completion claims had sometimes preceded realistic workflow testing and installed acceptance.

I would carry that lesson into acceptance design: identify the authentic producer, real parsed representation, ordinary host invocation, meaningful positive case, justified refusal case and responsible Manager's observable verdict before closing the requirement. Keep implementation, testing, commissioning, data availability and functional acceptance separate. An unavailable producer deserves a named compatibility decision, not indefinite additional consumer validation.

My own risk here is confirmation bias: selecting every defect because the principal suspects gatekeeping. I checked later repair and acceptance evidence, preserved scope-qualified completions, and did not turn provider refusals, usage-limit interruptions or valid safety controls into proof of an anti-trading motive. The observable pattern is repeated weak linkage between commissioned outcomes and engineering acceptance, with later corrections. These records cannot reveal a model's internal causal state or prove its training origin.
